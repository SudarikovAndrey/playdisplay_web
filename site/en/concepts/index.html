Concepts

Twenty-eight concepts ready to be built: the idea, what it solves, how it works and where it applies. Not a portfolio — formats you can take into a project.

Museums

  • Portal Into a Space That Doesn't Exist Yet — When you need to show an unbuilt project, a vanished world or an unreachable space — and let people explore it on their own.
  • See What's Hidden Inside — When what's inside an object matters more than its outer shell.
  • Visitor Becomes Part of the Exhibition — When an exhibition needs a personal takeaway that people will want to keep and show to others.
  • Exhibition as Spatial Composition — When you want to break out of the usual row of screens and build the space around one visual idea.
  • A Big Journey for a Big Audience — When dozens or hundreds of viewers have to step inside the same story at the same time.
  • Team Quest Around One Table — When visitors need a shared task — and each of them a role of their own.
  • Immersion Starts at the Door — When the first impression has to set the mood for the whole space, instantly.
  • A Wall That Lives With the Space — When a large architectural volume has to hold both a permanent identity and content that keeps changing.
  • A Ship's Story Unfolds Around Its Model — When a physical exhibit needs movement, scale and events added to it.

Brands

  • Complex Object, One Synchronized Show — When a large group has to move quickly through a dense product presentation.
  • Future Building, Right Above the Model — When a buyer needs to see a future property in more depth than renders and brochures allow.
  • Property at the Scale of Being There — When a project has to be shown to a whole group with a sense of full immersion.
  • Showroom That Sells a Way of Life — When the sales office has to be part of the project's brand and back up the work of the sales team.
  • A Physical Object Comes to Life — When a scale model has to show not just a shape, but action, environment and the processes around it.
  • An Object That Looks Impossible — When a product or an exhibit has to own the room and be explored in three dimensions.
  • Show How a Space Will Live — When what you're selling is not an interior as a set of finishes, but the life that will happen inside it.
  • One Object Stops the Crowd — When you need a simple, physically convincing center of attention.
  • Screens Become Architecture — When a space needs a form of its own and content has to escape the standard rectangle.

City

  • Facade That Reacts to People — When an architectural surface has to become the main media object of the space.
  • The Space Starts Speaking — When an interior needs a live information layer built into the architecture itself.
  • The Street Gets a Window Into Somewhere Else — When passers-by need a reason to care about a place or an event before they've decided to go in.
  • Skill Through Action and Full Presence — When someone has to train or run missions in a controlled virtual environment.
  • Training Turns Into a Game — When people need a reason to move longer, more often and with more appetite.
  • Every Step Becomes Part of the Message — When an everyday route should turn into a visual and narrative event.
  • A Real Jump Lands You in a Digital World — When you need a spectacular attraction that reads at a glance and runs on real physical movement.

Future

  • Museum as a Working Space Base — When an educational subject has to become a sequence of personal missions.
  • Artwork Painted by a State of Mind — When you want to give visitors an unexpected way to see and express what's going on inside them.
  • A Space Mission for a Real Crew — When you need a flagship attraction with a long scenario, repeat visits and strong group dynamics.
