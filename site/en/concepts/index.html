Portal Into a Space That Doesn't Exist Yet

When you need to show an unbuilt project, a vanished world or an unreachable space — and let people explore it on their own.

What it solves

  • Shows ideas and objects before they physically exist.
  • Turns a presentation into self-guided exploration.
  • Makes the scale, structure and atmosphere of a space easy to grasp.

How it works

A digital portal opens on a phone, a tablet or AR glasses. The visitor steps closer, shifts the angle and literally looks through into another world.

I'm already inside something that so far exists only as an idea.

Mechanics

  • Full-scale AR portal
  • Interactive points of interest
  • Spatial audio
  • Free movement
  • AI guide or shared mode — optional

Why this way

Video walks the viewer along a route someone else chose. An AR portal hands over the controls. People decide where to look, what to examine and how fast to move — and the digital model starts to feel like a personal discovery. In education the same format opens the door to historical eras, natural environments, the inner workings of the body or objects in deep space.

Where it applies

Real estate development • museums • exhibitions • education • tourism • corporate showrooms

What it can show

Residential developments, interiors, lost architecture, historical events, natural environments, machinery, vehicles, scientific models and fictional worlds.

See What's Hidden Inside

When what's inside an object matters more than its outer shell.

What it solves

  • Explains complex engineering without taking the object apart.
  • Links a physical model to the processes running inside it.
  • Makes a product's advantages visible at a glance.

How it works

The visitor points a tablet at a real object or a scale model. On screen the shell turns transparent: mechanisms, flows, inner layers and the links between processes come into view.

I can see how it's built and why it works.

Mechanics

  • Object recognition
  • Precisely registered 3D graphics
  • Animation of internal processes
  • Interactive layers
  • Handheld or fixed screens

Why this way

Complex systems are hard to explain in words and diagrams. When digital graphics lock precisely onto the real object, people see the form and the operating principle at the same time. The path from curiosity to understanding gets much shorter, and the object can be revealed layer by layer.

Where it applies

Industry • technology museums • education • medicine • showrooms • exhibitions

What it can show

Aircraft, cars, machine tools, buildings, building systems, the human body, geological strata, historical artifacts.

Visitor Becomes Part of the Exhibition

When an exhibition needs a personal takeaway that people will want to keep and show to others.

What it solves

  • Turns a viewer into a participant.
  • Creates a natural magnet — and a natural sharing point.
  • Extends contact with the exhibition far beyond its walls.

How it works

A person looks into a mirror. A few seconds later the reflection becomes a portrait — in the style of an artist, a period or the exhibition's own visual language.

This artwork was made out of me, just now.

Mechanics

  • Mirror screen
  • Camera and generative processing
  • Several artistic styles
  • QR code or direct delivery of the result
  • Queue and repeat-visit scenario

Why this way

Personalization grabs attention instantly: people find themselves inside the exhibit. The short wait builds anticipation, and the finished portrait becomes a tangible continuation of the visit — saved, sent to friends, posted.

Where it applies

Museums • art exhibitions • brand zones • festivals • learning spaces

What it can show

Styles of specific artists, periods, cultures, scientific themes, brands and events. Portraits can be made for couples, families and whole groups.

Exhibition as Spatial Composition

When you want to break out of the usual row of screens and build the space around one visual idea.

What it solves

  • Gives the exhibition an architecture of its own.
  • Binds several screens into a single narrative.
  • Puts all content under one control console.

How it works

Screens, pylons and curved surfaces form one continuous composition. Content flows from plane to plane, and the viewer reads the exhibit as a single object.

I'm standing inside an interface, not in front of a bank of monitors.

Mechanics

  • Screens of non-standard geometry
  • Synchronized content
  • Concave central screen
  • Control console
  • Lighting scenario

Why this way

Scattered displays force people to switch between separate messages. One spatial composition holds attention and lets you build real dramaturgy: from the big picture to the detail, from passive watching to choosing and exploring.

Where it applies

Exhibitions • museums • showrooms • presentation centers • corporate spaces

What it can show

Shape, number of modules, control setup and content all adjust to the subject, the floor area and the expected visitor flow.

A Big Journey for a Big Audience

When dozens or hundreds of viewers have to step inside the same story at the same time.

What it solves

  • Delivers presence at scale.
  • Shows places and events at a scale that convinces.
  • Works as the climax of a route or as a standalone attraction.

How it works

A concave screen wraps the field of view and moves the audience into a virtual environment: a city, a historical era, a natural landscape or a world of technology.

The room disappeared and we were there.

Mechanics

  • Large concave LED screen
  • Panoramic content
  • Spatial audio
  • Interactive or linear scenario
  • Group viewing

Why this way

Peripheral vision is what makes presence feel real. A curved screen fills it and hides the edges of the image. That's why even a large group reads the content as a space rather than a film on a wall.

Where it applies

Museums • visitor centres • tourist centers • corporate showrooms • learning spaces

What it can show

Historical reconstructions, city journeys, expeditions into nature, industry, science, space, projects still to be built.

Team Quest Around One Table

When visitors need a shared task — and each of them a role of their own.

What it solves

  • Replaces solo viewing with a shared experience.
  • Exercises communication and decision-making.
  • Holds a group through a long scenario.

How it works

Six players take places around an interactive table. Each has a personal interface, and the outcome depends on how well the group combines its decisions.

Nobody gets through this alone — we have to act together.

Mechanics

A shared goal turns strangers into a team within seconds. Every individual action affects the common result, so attention is held by social dynamics as much as by content. The scenario blends learning, play and discussion without strain.

Where it applies

Museums • science centers • corporate training • entertainment venues • exhibitions

Science missions, running a city, ecology, history, space, corporate challenges and pure entertainment quests.

Immersion Starts at the Door

When the first impression has to set the mood for the whole space, instantly.

What it solves

  • Turns the entrance into a scene in its own right.
  • Delivers the brand's atmosphere before the main route even begins.
  • Creates a strong visual landmark.

How it works

LED surfaces are built into the frame of doors and passages. Water, light, fire or abstract graphics stream around the opening and change what crossing it feels like.

I crossed a line and came out in another world.

Mechanics

  • LED screens integrated into the architecture
  • Content shaped to the opening
  • Light and sound
  • Response to approach
  • Indoor or outdoor build

Why this way

Walking through a doorway is already a natural change of state. When architecture underlines that moment with movement and light, people arrive emotionally prepared for what comes next. The entrance becomes part of the narrative instead of a piece of infrastructure.

Where it applies

Museums • offices • showrooms • exhibitions • shopping centers • public spaces

What it can show

From a compact doorway portal to a large facade installation. Content can follow the season, the event, the brand or the story of the place.

A Wall That Lives With the Space

When a large architectural volume has to hold both a permanent identity and content that keeps changing.

What it solves

  • Brings a blank wall to life.
  • Connects a commemorative object to the organization's current life.
  • Gives the interior a strong center.

How it works

A solid monument or display merges with a large screen. The permanent part holds the core of the story; the digital part carries events, people, data and new meaning.

The story of this place is continuing right now.

Mechanics

  • Solid monolith
  • Large LED screen
  • Updatable content
  • Infographics and archive
  • Event scenarios

Why this way

A fully digital wall loses its sense of weight fast; a fully material one stops developing. Two layers together give both permanence and life: the place keeps its symbol and still shows something new.

Where it applies

Universities • business schools • museums • corporate centers • public buildings

What it can show

A founders' wall, company history, achievements, scientific discoveries, an events calendar and a living community archive.

When you need a simple, physically convincing center of attention.

What it solves

  • Lifts a product or an exhibit out of the informational noise.

The object floats in the air, turning slowly. Form, light and material are chosen to make its weight look utterly real.

Exhibitions • museums • window displays • showrooms • product launches

A stone, a product, an artifact, a logo, a model of a planet, an engineered component or an art object.
