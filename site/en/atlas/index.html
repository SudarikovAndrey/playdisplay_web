Atlas: how we think about space

Fifty principles, techniques and observations the studio works by. Not a manifesto for its own sake: this is what we lean on when deciding what an object will be.

Principles

  1. Designers don't decorate — they find the form of the solutionExpression is never laid on top of function. It grows out of the problem itself: how people behave, what the space is like, what the project's limits are, and which technologies are chosen.
  2. The future already exists as optionsInspiration is the moment a designer notices one of the possible futures before anyone else does. Value appears when that option can be turned into a real object, space or experience.
  3. Flow demands total attentionStrong solutions rarely arrive in passing. The designer goes all the way into the problem, notices connections, discards the obvious answers and holds the idea until it takes exact form.
  4. Technology guarantees nothingThe same technology can create a powerful experience or stay expensive equipment. Value appears when it becomes part of a precise idea.
  5. Every constraint makes the idea strongerBudget, schedule, architecture, safety and existing equipment apply the pressure that an exact solution is born out of.
  6. Space is a languageLight, scale, path, sound and movement tell the story before any text does.
  7. The best ideas look inevitableOnce it is built, it seems there was no other way. That effect appears when a difficult problem has been solved with real precision.
  8. Complexity belongs backstageThe more natural an interaction feels, the more work is usually hidden inside the project.
  9. We design behaviorScreens, projectors and software can be bought. The real work is designing how people will move, stop, wonder and interact.
  10. The experience begins before the first stepA project starts with anticipation and ends with a memory. The entrance, the queue and the first glance already shape how the experience is received.
  11. A good idea brings people togetherA strong project starts a conversation: people point out details to each other, take photos, laugh and come back together.
  12. Real magic leaves questionsWhen people want a second look, want to walk around it and check their own senses, the impression stays with them.
  13. Memory keeps moments you lived throughFacts fade fast. Personal experience, surprise and emotion go on living in memory.
  14. Surprise opens attentionSurprise creates a pause in which a person is ready to look, listen and explore further.
  15. The best technology disappearsWhen visitors talk about the experience and not the equipment, the technology has done its job.
  16. Design is a way of making decisionsDesign starts with the question of what a person should feel and do, then builds form, narrative and technology around that answer.
  17. Constraints are the best co-authorExpression often comes about because of a project's limits, not in spite of them.
  18. A good idea looks obvious once someone has had itPrecision hides the search behind it and makes the result feel natural.
  19. Creativity is the ability to see a connectionA strong idea joins together elements that until then were seen separately.
  20. Every project starts with a feelingFirst we decide what a person should feel. Then we build everything that helps them live it.

Technologies

  1. Virtual reality for trainingVR makes it safe to rehearse difficult and rare situations, repeat scenarios and get instant feedback.
  2. Generative graphicsThe image is created in real time and changes in response to a person, to data or to the state of the space.
  3. Computer visionCameras read movement, position and action, turning natural behavior into a way of controlling things.
  4. Spatial soundSound builds direction, depth and a sense of presence, tying a virtual event to a physical place.
  5. Sensor systemsMovement, touch, voice and body position become inputs for the scenario.

Observations

  1. People rarely retell factsBut they almost always retell the moments that surprised them.
  2. Architecture speaks firstPeople read scale, light, form and route before any text appears.
  3. Space can guide without signageLight, sound, perspective and movement suggest the next step on their own.
  4. Simplicity usually hides a lot of workThe more natural an interaction looks, the more carefully the scenario and the technology behind it were worked out.
  5. If people discuss the equipment, the idea has lostIf they discuss what they felt and lived through, the technology worked.
  6. A shared emotion is stronger than a private onePeople remember an experience better when they have shared it with others.
  7. Watching can be as good as taking partParticipants' reactions can become part of the show and draw in the people around them.
  8. A personal action changes what the experience is worthWhatever a person has influenced themselves feels more significant to them.

Techniques

  1. A space people want to photographA strong installation tells a person where to stop and what to do. The photograph becomes part of the narrative, and the experience travels further.
  2. Give adults their curiosity backPlay briefly removes status, job title and habitual seriousness. A person explores, tries things and enjoys discovery again.
  3. Magic is stronger than technologyVisitors care more about the moment of the impossible than about how it is done. A real object comes alive, an image appears in mid-air, familiar physics changes.
  4. Several technologies, one effectLight, sound, mechanics, graphics and sensors all work at once, but read as a single whole event.
  5. The visitor becomes a participantThe story turns personal when a visitor affects what happens through movement, voice or choice.
  6. Better togetherA shared task turns a group into a team and creates a memory they hold in common.
  7. The experience begins in the queueAn installation is worth watching from the outside, not only going through. Other people's reactions build the intrigue.
  8. Surprise first, explain afterThe first effect opens attention. After that a complex story can be told in real depth.
  9. The action has to feel naturalStep up, look in, touch, turn, jump — the clearer the first gesture, the faster a person enters the experience.
  10. The story continues after the exitA photograph, a game result, an image someone made or simply retelling it all extend the life of the experience.
  11. Every visitor goes through their own storyOne space can offer different routes, choices and depths of interaction.
  12. The real thing begins where the screens endWhen media become part of the architecture, the light, the objects and the movement, the space reads as one whole.
  13. Memory loves the unexpectedMoments that break expectations stay with people longer than a consistent explanation.

Research

  1. Virtual worlds instead of websitesA browser can become a space for travel, presentation, learning or acting together.
  2. Personal sound in a public spaceDirectional audio systems make individual sound scenarios possible without headphones.
  3. The dome as a windowA vertical hemisphere creates a different kind of immersion: you are not looking up, you are looking through an opening into another environment.
  4. Personalizing the spaceOne place can change its content, its language and its level of difficulty for the particular visitor in front of it.
